# 0601 — The business engine

**Status: DONE (3 October 2026). Save v38.** First ticket of v0.06 Business &
Advanced Wealth. Spec 393, 398–400, 404, 849–878, 1356.

## What it does

Assets → Businesses lists what you run (its name, what it made last year, what
it is worth to you) and, under it, a marketplace of the businesses you could
start. You must be 18 and hold enough: a type shows once your cash plus
portfolio reaches 60% of its startup cost (0602 changed this to net worth), and nothing on screen says which
"tier" that makes you. Opening takes the startup out of cash as a transfer
(`property`, not an expense) and puts a business on the books. You can run
three.

Twelve businesses (`packages/content/data/businesses.json`, generated by
`scripts/generate-businesses.py`): cleaning, landscaping, salon, café, fitness
studio, auto repair, restaurant, clothing store, HVAC, software studio,
accounting practice and a hotel. Startups run from $20,000 to $5.2 million;
nothing in a life today reaches the hotel. The rest of spec 396's list is 0602.

**One business's screen** (spec 1331): revenue, profit, cash in the till,
employees and price at the top; Customers (demand in three words, brand
reputation in five); a tap-track price slider from 70% to 140% of the going
rate in steps of five; a supplier choice (budget / standard / premium) for the
types where the product matters; payroll Low / Medium / High / Big Bucks;
hire, let go, and a switch for a manager who does both; how much of the owner
is in it; worth, a valuation band, Sell and Close.

## How a year works (`finance/businesses.ts`)

- **Demand** = the type's mature revenue × how far it has matured × the economy
  (scaled by how cyclical the trade is) × reputation × the owner's skill ×
  luck drawn once at opening × a yearly shock (bigger while young) ×
  (price ÷ quality)^−elasticity. Quality comes from supplier and pay.
- **Capacity** = what each worker can serve × (staff + the owner's hands). The
  business sells the smaller of the two. Too few staff means customers turned
  away; too many means idle ones.
- **Costs**: goods (fixed per unit, whatever the price), pay (with what
  turnover costs), overhead owed however it sells.
- **Profit is the owner's pay.** The business keeps three months of costs in
  its own till (spec 23: business income stays in the business) and pays the
  rest out as `business` income, taxed at the owner's marginal rate on salary
  plus draw and 7.65% self-employment tax on the draw. A loss comes out of the
  till, then out of the owner (a `property` transfer). An owner who cannot
  cover one loses the business: fittings sold, anything over paid to them.
- **Brand reputation** drifts toward what people get for what they pay. A
  better supplier or pay lifts it; a high price and a shop too busy to serve
  everyone lower it.
- **Staffing** is automatic by default, like a manager: hire a few when
  customers are turned away, let go when the work isn't there, never below what
  the doors need. Hiring or letting go by hand turns it off; the switch turns
  it back on.
- **Valuation** is the larger of the fittings (depreciated) and a multiple of
  the last three years' profit scaled by reputation, plus the till. A sale
  draws one buyer a year at 94% × exp(0.07z), less 5% to the broker; closing
  gets 40% of the fittings and the till.

## Where it reaches

Net worth counts businesses at their worth to the owner. At a death they are
sold at the expected offer and the heir receives the proceeds as a gift. The
summary's tax rate, `earnedIncomeOf` (car pricing) and `incomeOf` (underwriting)
all see the draw. Passive lives come out byte-identical: no business, no
`business` rows, `businesses: []`. Save v37 → v38 writes `businesses: []`.

## Calibration (what I tuned against, and what it still misses)

Mature owner pay at neutral luck, fully staffed, at the default settings:
roughly $40,000–$150,000 for the trades and shops, a few hundred thousand for
the hotel owner on a $5.2 million stake; margins of 7–23% by type. The best price for
every type falls between 85% and 120% of the going rate (inside the engine
the optimum is near 95%), and no payroll or supplier setting beats the default
by more than a third on average once the manager has staffed to match; Big
Bucks pays the owner less than Medium.

**Known gap, stated plainly.** Survival in the first five years comes out
around 81%, against 51% in the BLS table (80 / 69 / 51 / 35 at 1, 2, 5, 10
years). Early years are riskier than later ones, as they should be, but
nothing yet puts competitors, bad luck or a lost lease into a business's life.
0604 (events and competition) owns closing that gap; until then a player who
runs a business sensibly will find it more forgiving than the real world.

_0604 update:_ events and a rival took five-year survival from 81.5% to 78.7%
and it stayed there on purpose; see `0604-business-events-and-heirs.md`.

## Deferred, and why

License-gated trades (an HVAC business wants a trade license, an accounting
practice a CPA) and location-dependent availability remain unbuilt; 0602 added
the catalog and net-worth gate but not those qualification/location gates.
0603 built purchase-linked business loans and buying an existing business; it
did not build selling a business at auction or general cash borrowing against
one. Tax-loss offsets against a salary are not modelled: a loss is just a
loss. Sale proceeds are counted as income by `incomeOf` (finding 35) and are
not corrected here.

## Findings from this ticket

34 and 35 are in `v006-business-measurement.md`. New:

**36. `advanceYear` is not pure over its state.** Two calls from the same
state object give different results from the second year on, because
`state.rng` carries live streams that the first call mutates. A fresh game
built the same way twice is deterministic, which is what every test relies on
and what a player sees. Saves persist the seed **and live stream states**
through `rng.snapshot()` / `Rng.restore`; `persistence.test.ts` already checks
one year's continued timeline entries after a save. Neither establishes input
immutability or a full multi-year state replay. Those remain worth checking
before undo, a preview or autosave-replay (roadmap finding 36).
